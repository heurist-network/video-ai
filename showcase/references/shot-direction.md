# Shot direction

## Choose promotional graphics or a product demo

Use the user’s request to distinguish these treatments; social distribution alone does not turn
a product demo into a promotional graphic.

For a promotional graphic, compose around one message and one dominant visual. Do not
reconstruct a realistic interface by default. Remove minor labels, duplicate names,
timestamps, explanatory copy, decorative text, charts and controls unless they directly
carry that message. Make the key number or phrase large enough to read at social-feed
size. Keep secondary facts visibly subordinate. For example, a stock-price promo can
use a ticker, a headline price and a smaller signed dollar change without company names,
percentages, market headings or a price chart. Keep provenance in the surrounding board
notes; retain any qualification essential to the truth of the claim.

For an actual product demo, preserve the real layout, labels, states and interaction
sequence needed to understand the feature. Simplify by framing and selective emphasis,
not by inventing product behavior. Interface fidelity serves the demonstration; it is
not a requirement for promotional typography or composed marketing graphics.

## Show the action, not a slide about it

Designing storyboard shots is not creating a slideshow. Do not add editorial titles,
subtitles, small meta-descriptions, citations or reference links inside the film merely
to explain the shot. Put composition, provenance, capture dates and methodological
context in the storyboard's surrounding notes. Keep native UI text, chart labels,
units and other information necessary to understand the depicted action or data.
Do not remove a material qualification or required disclosure merely for cleanliness;
resolve its presentation with the director. Authored title cards are a deliberate
creative choice, not the default for every scene.

Each visual detail should establish the product, explain an interaction, guide attention
or carry a transition. If it does none of these, try removing it before embellishing it.

## Connect shots, not just entrances

Specify what survives each shot boundary: the same panel, a selected row, a cursor
click, a chart surface or a continuing camera move. A replay button and several isolated
fade-ins are not a continuous animatic. Describe the outgoing state, connecting motion
and incoming settled state in the board. Demonstrate them together only when needed
to judge a creative choice; a fully animated sequence is not required for board lock.

A useful treatment is a browser frame that establishes context, then recedes while its
content expands into the film. Keep chrome and content on separate layers, preserve the
content's spatial continuity, and use uniform camera scaling rather than stretching
text to fit a changing rectangle. This is an option when appropriate, not mandatory
macOS chrome for every product.

## Preserve the answer while editing its presentation

Removing editorial captions must not remove the product's answer. In an agent demo,
keep user and assistant roles distinct, show why one result leads to the next, and
place tables or charts inside the message that produced them. Preserve enough
source-backed explanation and conversational context for the viewer to follow the
sequence. Camera continuity alone cannot repair a missing logical connection.

Continuity does not require streaming the whole transcript or repeating the same
text-reveal effect before every chart. Demonstrate streaming where it establishes the
interaction; a later reply can lead directly into its inline chart when the user turn
and remaining context make the connection clear. After removing prose, close the
unused wait and reframe the chart without sacrificing the user-message reading hold,
chart reveal or settled inspection window. Do not replace removed prose with invented
connective claims.

Source fidelity and editorial quality are separate checks. A verbatim sentence can
still be repetitive, distracting or ill-suited to the film. Select or omit source
passages for clarity rather than defending every sentence because it is authentic.
Preserve meaning, role, order and material qualifications; do not silently rewrite
research or present separated excerpts as the complete uninterrupted answer. Keep
provenance and the editorial-selection boundary in the surrounding storyboard notes.

## Choreograph the cursor

Give each pointer movement a reason: approach a target, decelerate, pause briefly,
click, then show the consequence. Use restrained press/ring feedback and dismiss the
cursor when its job is done. Avoid decorative wandering, perpetual motion and a glowing
dot standing in for a recognizable pointer.

Measure cursor-tip alignment against the actual target under the camera transform.
Check both the click and an intermediate approach frame so a faulty measurement cannot
report a trivial zero. Typing, clicking and the resulting UI must follow the same clock.

## Keep backgrounds and branding subordinate

Begin with the product's palette and approved visual references. Texture is not a
requirement to add gradients, halos, grids or particles. A neutral surface with nearly
invisible static grain may provide enough depth. A grid behind a data grid can compete
with the content. When a treatment is rejected, compare a stripped-back still before
inventing a different decorative pattern; after repeated rejection, use a director-chosen
reference rather than continuing to guess.

Use the complete approved logo asset without stretching, redrawing or tacking on a
product suffix to invent a new lockup. Decide where branding belongs in the composition;
repeating a corner logo and a central logo is not automatically useful. Inspect the
actual asset and its placement, not just whether its file loaded.

## Make iteration cheaper than the build

Settle an opening and closing style frame before an extensive animation rewrite.
Add a representative transition study only when stills and motion descriptions are
insufficient to judge the treatment. Preserve readable holds while adding thinking beats,
row reveals or cell waves; propose a duration change rather than silently compressing
reading time. Brief workers with the approved reference, explicit freedom and fixed
constraints. The lead still owns reviewing visual choices; a technically valid worker
result is not a taste approval.
